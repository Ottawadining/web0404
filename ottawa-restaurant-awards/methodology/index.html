Methodology

How the Ranking Works

Ottawa's Most Awarded Restaurants is intended to measure documented culinary achievement rather than personal preference.

To create the ranking, we reviewed qualifying recognition received by Ottawa restaurants and chefs during the twenty calendar years from January 1, 2007 through December 31, 2026. Every restaurant is evaluated using the same scoring system.

Which Restaurants Are Eligible?

Restaurants must be currently operating in Ottawa at the time of publication to appear in the ranking. Closed restaurants are not eligible for the current Top 10.

However, individual chef achievements may remain relevant when the chef is currently leading an eligible Ottawa restaurant. This distinction is important. Restaurant-specific recognition belongs to the restaurant. Individual chef recognition belongs to the chef.

For example, if a chef won a culinary competition while operating a restaurant that has since closed, the individual achievement may be credited as part of the chef's record at their current restaurant. Whenever this occurs, the original year and historical context are identified.

Why 2007–2026?

The study covers exactly twenty calendar years. Only achievements occurring between January 1, 2007 and December 31, 2026 are eligible for points. Achievements before 2007 may be mentioned as historical context but never contribute points.

Beckta received important national recognition before 2007. It is part of the restaurant's history but is excluded from this ranking because it falls outside the study period.

What We Don't Count

The ranking intentionally excludes many common restaurant accolades:

  • Local readers' choice awards
  • Google ratings
  • Yelp
  • TripAdvisor
  • OpenTable awards
  • Paid restaurant awards
  • Wine Spectator restaurant awards
  • Generic media mentions
  • Local "Best Restaurant" articles
  • Social media popularity
  • Public voting competitions
  • Ottawa Chef of the Year
  • Three Diamond CAA/AAA recognition
  • Awards outside 2007–2026

La Liste is also excluded, as no Ottawa restaurant qualified during the study period. The objective is to keep the dataset concentrated on substantial professional, national and international recognition.

How Ties Are Resolved

When two or more restaurants receive identical scores, the following procedure applies in order:

1. Highest-level culinary achievement. The restaurant possessing the most significant individual achievement ranks first.

2. Number of qualifying achievements. If restaurants remain tied, the restaurant with more qualifying achievements ranks first.

3. Highest Canada's 100 Best placement. If both restaurants have Canada's 100 Best recognition and remain tied, their highest historical placement is used.

If restaurants still cannot be separated objectively, they may share a ranking.

This ranking does not attempt to determine which Ottawa restaurant serves the best food today.

It measures documented achievement over a defined twenty-year period. A younger restaurant naturally has had fewer opportunities to accumulate recognition than a restaurant operating throughout the study period.

Scores therefore represent historical achievement, not a prediction of current dining quality.
